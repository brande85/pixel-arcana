✨ Understanding Tarot Through Gaming ✨

What is Tarot?

Tarot is a symbolic system of 78 cards used for reflection and insight. Each card represents universal human experiences—archetypes that appear across cultures and stories. In Pixel Arcana, we explore those timeless patterns through the lens of video games.

📚 The Deck Structure

🎮 Major Arcana: The Boss Fights

Cards 0-21: The Fool through The World

These are the big moments—life's major lessons and transformative experiences. Think of them like boss fights in a game: significant encounters that fundamentally change your journey.

💡 Example: The Tower represents sudden upheaval—like when Aerith dies in Final Fantasy VII, completely changing the story's direction.

⚔️ Minor Arcana: The Daily Quests

Four suits of 14 cards each (Ace through King)

These are the day-to-day experiences—smaller challenges, victories, and moments that make up the actual journey. Like side quests and regular encounters, they're how you live the adventure.

💡 Example: Two of Cups represents partnership—like Cody and May in It Takes Two rebuilding their relationship through cooperation, learning their strengths complement each other perfectly.

Major Arcana: The Fool's Journey

The 22 Major Arcana cards tell a story called The Fool's Journey—from innocence (The Fool) to enlightenment (The World). It's humanity's hero's journey, the path from not-knowing to wisdom.

🌟 The Journey Begins

Think of it like a video game campaign: you start as a novice with everything to learn, face increasingly complex challenges, confront your shadow, and eventually master the game. Each Major Arcana card is a checkpoint in that journey.

Minor Arcana: The Four Suits

The Minor Arcana is divided into four suits, each representing a different aspect of life. Think of them as your character's core stats: Fire (action), Water (emotion), Air (mind), Earth (body).

🔥 Wands (Fire Element)

Energy: Passion, creativity, action, willpower, inspiration

Questions: What drives you? What are you building? What fires you up?

Gaming Parallel: Your quest log, main objectives, creative projects, speedruns

💡 Example: Ace of Wands represents new beginnings—like Link receiving his first sword in the original Zelda, the spark of adventure and pure creative potential ready to be explored.

💧 Cups (Water Element)

Energy: Emotions, relationships, love, intuition, dreams

Questions: How do you feel? Who matters to you? What does your heart want?

Gaming Parallel: Character relationships, emotional story beats, romance options, found family

💡 Example: Eight of Cups represents walking away—like Journey's robed figure leaving desert ruins behind to climb toward a distant mountain, spiritual pilgrimage toward something more meaningful.

🗡️ Swords (Air Element)

Energy: Thoughts, truth, mental clarity, conflict, communication, decisions

Questions: What do you think? What truth must you face? What decision awaits?

Gaming Parallel: Moral choices, strategy, difficult decisions, puzzle-solving, truth-seeking

💡 Example: Two of Swords represents difficult choices—like picking your starter Pokémon, a decision between equally appealing options that sets your entire journey in motion.

🪙 Pentacles (Earth Element)

Energy: Material world, work, body, resources, practical matters, physical reality

Questions: What do you have? What are you building? What's tangible and real?

Gaming Parallel: Resources, crafting, character builds, grinding, inventory management

💡 Example: Eight of Pentacles represents mastery through practice—like grinding combos in a fighting game, dedicating yourself to perfecting your craft through repetition and focus.

🔢 The Numbered Journey (Ace through 10)

👑 Court Cards: The Character Classes

Each suit has four "people" cards representing different approaches to that suit's energy:

Pages: Students, messengers, new ventures

Enthusiastic but inexperienced—like starting a new game

Knights: Action-takers, champions, extremes

Passionate and impulsive—rushing into battle

Queens: Nurturers, masters, internalized

Mature and receptive—wise support characters

Kings: Leaders, authority, externalized

Commanding and directive—guild leaders and mentors

Reading Your Cards

Drawing a card isn't about fortune-telling—it's about reflection. The cards give you a lens to examine your life from a different angle.

🎯 How to Interpret

  1. Look at the card's meaning: What archetype does it represent?
  2. Notice which game appeared: What does that game's story tell you?
  3. Read the keywords: Which ones resonate with your situation?
  4. Check if it's reversed: Shadow side, blocked energy, or alternate perspective?
  5. Consider the position: Past, present, or future? Mind, body, or spirit?
  6. Trust your intuition: What does this make you think of in your life right now?

💡 Example Reading

You drew: The Hermit (The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild)

  • Meaning: Solitude, inner wisdom, introspection, soul-searching
  • Game context: Link wakes alone after 100 years, exploring vast wilderness in isolation, finding shrines and wisdom through solitary journey
  • Keywords that stand out: "Withdrawal, inner guidance, enlightenment through isolation"
  • Question: Where in your life do you need to retreat inward right now?
  • Reflection: Are you avoiding necessary alone time? Or have you isolated yourself too much?

🔗 Reading Combinations

When multiple cards appear together, notice patterns:

  • Multiple cards from one suit: Strong focus on that area (lots of Cups = emotional processing)
  • Lots of Major Arcana: Big life themes and major transitions happening
  • Court cards together: People in your life or different personality aspects interacting
  • Reversed cards clustered: Blocked energy or significant shadow work needed
  • Same numbers repeating: That stage of the journey is emphasized across multiple areas

Understanding Reversals

In traditional tarot, when a card appears upside-down, it offers a different perspective on that archetype's energy.

🔄 What Does Reversed Mean?

There are several ways to interpret reversed cards:

  • Shadow side: The challenging or difficult aspect of that card's energy
  • Blocked energy: The card's power is present but can't flow freely
  • Internalized: The energy is turned inward rather than expressed outward
  • Opposite: The absence or reversal of the upright meaning
  • Invitation to look deeper: There's more to explore beneath the surface

🎮 Reversals in Pixel Arcana

In this deck, reversed cards feature different games that explore the shadow or alternate expression of that archetype.

💡 Example:
Four of Cups Upright represents apathy—like Kaim in Lost Odyssey, emotionally numb after a thousand years of loss, withdrawing from connections because he's closed himself off behind emotional scar tissue.

Four of Cups Reversed represents awakening—like Neku in The World Ends With You removing his headphones and finally opening his world, choosing connection over isolation, seeing the opportunities that were there all along.

Same archetype, different expression—showing both the light and shadow of community and friendship.

⚙️ Should You Use Reversals?

This is entirely personal preference!

✓ With Reversals:
  • More nuance and depth
  • Addresses shadow work directly
  • 78 more games to discover
Without Reversals:
  • Simpler, easier to learn
  • Focus on positive framing
  • Still get full meanings

You can enable or disable reversals in Settings. Many experienced readers only use upright cards—it's totally valid!

Tarot Glossary

Quick reference for common tarot terms:

Archetype:

Universal pattern or symbol that appears across cultures and stories

Court Cards:

The "people" cards—Pages, Knights, Queens, and Kings in each suit

Major Arcana:

The 22 trump cards (0-21) representing big life themes and major turning points

Minor Arcana:

The 56 suit cards representing day-to-day situations and experiences

Pips:

The numbered cards, Ace through Ten in each suit

Querent:

The person receiving the reading (that's you!)

Reversed:

A card appearing upside-down, offering shadow or alternate perspective

Spread:

A layout pattern for multiple cards (like Past-Present-Future)

Suit:

One of four categories: Wands (Fire), Cups (Water), Swords (Air), Pentacles (Earth)

The Fool's Journey:

The narrative arc of the Major Arcana from innocence (The Fool) to enlightenment (The World)

Upright:

A card appearing right-side up in its standard orientation

Shadow Work:

Exploring the hidden, uncomfortable, or rejected parts of yourself
